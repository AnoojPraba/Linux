# 01_BehavioralAndLeadershipInterviewPrep

Foundations of the behavioral round for a senior candidate: STAR, the five stories to have ready, one worked production-incident answer, and how the round is actually scored.

## Files
- `NOTES.md` - (93 lines) sections: STAR format; Stories a 15-year candidate should have ready; Worked example: "Tell me about a time you led a production incident"; How this round actually goes; Meta-tip

## How to use this note
- Pick 4-5 real stories (disagreement, incident, mentoring, pushing back on a deadline, cross-team influence) and write each in STAR with "I" language and concrete numbers from your own history.
- Practise follow-up drilling aloud: "what did YOU do versus the team", "what would you do differently", "what if the reviewer was unavailable".
- Reshape one flexible story for several prompts (failure, pressure, bad news) instead of memorising one story per question.
- Notes are scaffolds: the stories, numbers and outcomes must come from your own real experience; rewrite every example in your voice before an interview.

## Key trade-offs / interview angles
- STAR: Situation, Task, Action (what YOU did and why), Result with measurable impact and learning.
- Scored on ownership, judgment under pressure, technical depth and structured communication.
- Senior failure mode: team-diffuse "we" answers; interviewers probe for individual contribution.
- The worked example (payment connection-pool leak, targeted hotfix vs rollback) is an illustration of specificity, not a script to reuse.
- Expect 30-45 minute rounds, 3-5 minutes of uninterrupted opening, 1-2 behavioral rounds at staff/principal level.

## Gotchas
- The notes are scaffolds: the worked example and all numbers are illustrative; your own stories, facts and outcomes must come from your real experience.
