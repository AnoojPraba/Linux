# People Leadership: Hiring, Performance, Delegation, Conflict

Builds on `01_BehavioralAndLeadershipInterviewPrep` (STAR, story categories). At 15+
years, interviewers (especially for staff/principal/EM roles) probe how you grow
people and teams, not just your own output. Use YOUR real examples; the structures
below are scaffolds - never invent facts.

## What the interviewer is scoring
Scope and impact beyond yourself, judgment under ambiguity, communication,
ownership, raising the bar on the team (hiring, mentoring, standards), handling
conflict/underperformance with empathy AND accountability, results with metrics.

## 1. Hiring
- **Process you can describe:** define the role + level rubric first (what "senior"
  means: scope, autonomy, technical depth, influence) -> structured loop (coding,
  design, behavioral, role-specific) with independent written feedback before the
  debrief -> calibrated bar ("would I want this person on my team in 2 years?") ->
  decision with evidence, not vibes.
- **Interviewing well:** consistent questions, probe for signal not trivia, avoid
  bias (similar-to-me, halo, anchoring on first impression), leave time for the
  candidate's questions, sell honestly.
- **Behaviors to cite:** raised the bar (said no to a likable but under-leveled
  candidate), trained interviewers (shadow/reverse-shadow), fixed a pipeline
  (diverse sourcing, shortened time-to-offer), closed a candidate (understood
  motivations: growth, scope, mission, comp).
- Pitfalls to avoid in answers: "I just go with my gut", hiring clones, no rubric.

## 2. Onboarding and growing people
- 30/60/90-day plan, a buddy, early small win, clear expectations, context (why
  not only what). Mentor vs coach vs sponsor: mentors advise; coaches ask questions;
  **sponsors** put your name forward for opportunities.
- Growth conversations: strengths-based plan tied to the person's goals and the
  team's needs; stretch assignments with safety nets; delegate OUTCOMES not tasks.
- **Delegation (levels):** tell -> sell -> consult -> agree -> advise -> inquire
  -> delegate fully. Match to the person's skill/will; stay accountable, remove
  blockers, check in on a cadence that tapers; resist taking the keyboard back.
- Feedback: **SBI** (Situation-Behavior-Impact) in near-real-time, privately for
  corrective and publicly for positive; specific, observable, about behavior not
  character; ask for their view; agree on next steps. Radical candor (care
  personally + challenge directly) avoids ruinous empathy and obnoxious aggression.

## 3. Performance management (underperformer story)
Structure that reads as fair and effective:
1. **Diagnose:** skill gap, will/motivation gap, unclear expectations, wrong fit,
   personal circumstances, or a systemic issue (bad tooling, overloaded team)?
2. **Be explicit and early:** private conversation with concrete examples and the
   expected standard; document; ask what's in the way.
3. **Support plan:** specific goals, timeline (e.g. 30-60-90 days), resources
   (pairing, training, scoped tasks), regular check-ins; involve HR/manager as
   policy requires.
4. **Outcome:** improvement (celebrate), role change, or a respectful exit with
   dignity. Protect the team's morale and delivery meanwhile.
- Metric-framed result: "within 8 weeks X delivered Y independently" or "we agreed it
  wasn't the right role and transitioned them; team velocity recovered".
- Pitfalls: surprising someone at review time, public criticism, ignoring it
  (the team notices and resents it), only describing punishment.
- Also cover **high performers** (retention, stretch, recognition, not overloading)
  and **brilliant jerks** (behavior standards apply to everyone; cost to team).

## 4. Conflict and difficult stakeholders
- **Between engineers:** hear each side separately, find the underlying interests
  (not positions), agree on decision criteria/data, time-box, decide-and-commit
  (disagree and commit), follow up on relationship. Escalate only after
  trying direct resolution.
- **With product/leadership on scope/deadline:** surface trade-offs (scope/time/
  quality/cost), give options with consequences, quantify risk, propose a phased
  plan, document the decision; support it fully once made.
- **With another team:** align on shared goal, make the cost of inaction visible,
  offer to help/own part of the work, use data, build the relationship before you
  need it, escalate with the other leader informed, not as an ambush.
- **When you were wrong:** own it quickly, fix, explain what you learned and what
  process changed (blameless).

## 5. Leading through change and ambiguity
- Communicate the why, the plan, what's unknown, and when you'll update; involve
  people early (reduces resistance); small reversible steps; measure.
- Reorgs, layoffs, attrition: be honest within constraints, protect critical
  knowledge (docs, pairing), re-establish priorities, care for the remaining team.
- Setting direction without authority (staff-level): write the proposal/RFC, build
  coalitions, prototype, find a sponsor, show data, make adopting easy.

## 6. Team health and culture
Psychological safety (people admit mistakes and ask questions), blameless
postmortems, clear ownership, sustainable on-call, code review norms, tech-debt
budget, recognition, 1:1s with the person's agenda first, skip-levels, retros that
produce actions that actually get done, team metrics (DORA: deploy frequency,
lead time, change fail rate, MTTR; plus satisfaction/attrition) used as signals,
not to rank individuals.

## Senior interviewer Q&A (prompts and answer shape)
**Q: Tell me about a time you had to manage out or coach up an underperformer.**
A shape: situation + expected standard -> your diagnosis -> conversation(s) and
plan with specifics -> support given -> outcome with a number and what you'd do
differently (e.g. act sooner).

**Q: How do you decide who to hire?**
A: Rubric by level, structured evidence from each interviewer, calibrate on
scope/ownership/learning ability not just trivia, bar-raising mindset, check
for gaps vs team needs, references; I'd rather leave a seat empty than lower
the bar.

**Q: How do you give difficult feedback?**
A: Prepare facts, SBI format, in private and soon after the event, ask for their
perspective, agree on actions, follow up and acknowledge improvement. Example with
a result.

**Q: How do you develop senior engineers who want to become staff/managers?**
A: Clarify the path and expectations, give increasing scope (lead a project across
teams), sponsor visibility, coach on influence/writing, let them try management
via a lead role/tech-lead-manager trial with a way back.

**Q: Describe a conflict with a peer or manager and how it resolved.**
A: Pick a real disagreement about substance (not personality), show curiosity about
their constraints, data-driven decision, disagree-and-commit, relationship
intact afterward, measurable outcome.

**Q: How do you keep a team motivated through a long, unglamorous project?**
A: Tie work to impact, break into visible milestones, rotate the toil, celebrate
progress, protect from scope churn, be honest about the timeline, and shield
the team from noise.

**Q: How do you handle a team member who is a great engineer but difficult to work with?**
A: Treat behavior as a performance dimension: concrete feedback with impact on
others, clear expectations, coaching/support, consequences if no change; don't
trade team health for individual output long-term.

**Q: How do you manage up and across?**
A: Regular concise updates (status, risks, asks), no surprises, bring options not
just problems, understand the leader's goals, build trust before escalations.
